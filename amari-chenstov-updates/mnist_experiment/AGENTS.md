# MNIST continual-learning experiment

This experiment tests whether linearized Fisher updates (LFUs) improve Fisher tracking and EWC continual learning when the true parameter moves continuously. Follow the shared scientific and artifact rules in [../AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) and the derivation in [../mathematical_overview.ipynb](../mathematical_overview.ipynb).

Do not implement a large Cartesian experiment immediately. Build and validate the experiment in the stages specified below, using immutable replica artifacts so later compute extends rather than replaces earlier evidence.

## Scientific model

The per-observation loss is a negative log likelihood up to an additive constant:

$$
L(X;\theta)=-\ell(X;\theta)+\mathrm{const}.
$$

Consequently, the loss gradient and score satisfy $g=-s$. For each $\theta$, there is one Fisher information matrix:

$$
\mathcal I(\theta)
=
\mathbb E_\theta[s(X;\theta)s(X;\theta)^T]
=
\mathbb E_\theta[g(X;\theta)g(X;\theta)^T].
$$

Let $M_i=1$ indicate a digit 9 observation and $M_i=0$ indicate a digit from 0 through 8. The environmental control is

$$
p=\mathbb P(M_i=1).
$$

Changing $p$ moves the true parameter implicitly:

$$
p\longmapsto\theta^\star(p)\longmapsto\mathcal I(\theta^\star(p)).
$$

The experiment treats this as the classical problem of estimating a moving true parameter. Do not add a second distribution argument to $\mathcal I$ or redefine the target as a separate empirical Fisher. Finite score samples produce an empirical estimator of the unique likelihood Fisher.

The original learning process estimates $\theta^\star(p)$ with $\widehat\theta_t$ and chooses $u_t=\widehat\theta_{t+1}-\widehat\theta_t$. The auxiliary Fisher process estimates $\mathcal I(\widehat\theta_t)$ along that realized path.

## Data trajectory

Each replica traverses a complete deterministic grid from $p=0$ to $p=1$:

$$
p_t=\frac{t}{K-1},\qquad t=0,\ldots,K-1.
$$

Expose both of these as required configuration or command-line arguments:

- `num_p_steps`: the number $K$ of points in the trajectory;
- `samples_per_step`: the number of observations processed at each $p_t$.

Within the $M=0$ component, preserve one fixed conditional distribution over digits 0 through 8 for the entire experiment. The default should use their conditional empirical MNIST frequencies; allow an explicitly configured balanced alternative. Changing $p$ must only change the mixture proportion assigned to digit 9.

Treat `num_p_steps` and `samples_per_step` as experimental variables. Analyze:

- a factorial grid that estimates their separate and interacting effects;
- compute-matched slices where `num_p_steps * samples_per_step` is approximately constant.

All estimator conditions in one paired replica must receive the same sampled class sequence and same selected MNIST observations whenever their paths are intentionally shared. Preserve the full trajectory, including $p=0$ and $p=1$, but exclude endpoints from finite-difference stencils and primary interior geometric summaries.

## Model

Use the complete trainable network when calculating scores, HVPs, and Fisher representations. Do not restrict the Fisher to the output head.

The default network consumes the original $28\times28$ image without external downsampling:

1. `Conv2d(1, 4, kernel_size=3, padding=1)`;
2. `SiLU`;
3. `AvgPool2d(2)`;
4. `Conv2d(4, 6, kernel_size=3, padding=1)`;
5. `SiLU`;
6. `AdaptiveAvgPool2d((2, 2))`;
7. flatten;
8. `Linear(24, 10)`.

With biases enabled, this network has exactly 512 trainable parameters:

$$
40+222+250=512.
$$

Assert the parameter count in code. Do not add batch normalization, dropout, ReLU, or max pooling to the canonical model. SiLU and average pooling preserve the smooth, deterministic derivative structure needed by the LFU analysis.

Use ordinary cross-entropy as the negative log likelihood. Do not apply a separate softmax before the loss.

Training may use the configured floating-point dtype, but accumulate dense Fisher matrices, reference matrices, eigenspectra, and scalar error metrics in `float64` unless a test explicitly studies precision.

## Parameter ordering

Define one stable flattening order for all trainable parameters and use it everywhere:

- flattened scores and HVPs;

- dense, diagonal, and low-rank representations;

Record parameter names, shapes, offsets, and total dimension in every run manifest. Fail rather than silently loading an artifact with a different ordering.

## Lagged LFU schedule

Suppose the model has arrived at $\theta_t$ after applying

$$
u_{t-1}=\theta_t-\theta_{t-1}.
$$

For a batch of $m$ observations $X_{t,b}$ at $\theta_t$, calculate per-sample loss gradients and HVPs:

$$
g_{t,b}=\nabla_\theta L(X_{t,b};\theta_t),
$$

$$
H_{t,b}=\nabla_\theta^2L(X_{t,b};\theta_t)u_{t-1}.
$$

The direct Fisher observation is

$$
Z_t=\frac1m\sum_{b=1}^m g_{t,b}g_{t,b}^T.
$$

The Amari-Chentsov-only contribution, in the loss-gradient convention, is

$$
\widehat\Delta_{C,t}
=
-\frac1m\sum_{b=1}^m
(u_{t-1}^Tg_{t,b})g_{t,b}g_{t,b}^T.
$$

The residual contribution is

$$
\widehat\Delta_{R,t}
=
\frac1m\sum_{b=1}^m
\left(
H_{t,b}g_{t,b}^T+g_{t,b}H_{t,b}^T
\right).
$$

The full LFU contribution is

$$
\widehat\Delta_t^{\mathrm{lag}}
=
\widehat\Delta_{C,t}+\widehat\Delta_{R,t}.
$$

For one sample this admits the signed rank-two factorization

$$
\widehat\Delta_{t,b}^{\mathrm{lag}}
=
U_{t,b}B_{t,b}U_{t,b}^T,
$$

$$
U_{t,b}=[g_{t,b},H_{t,b}],
\qquad
B_{t,b}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
-(u_{t-1}^Tg_{t,b})&1\\
1&0
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Apply the configured estimator's correction, blend it with the direct observation, and only then project or compress. In schema-v7 controller runs, the same predictable adaptation weight used by EWC controls this blend:

$$
A_t
=
(1-\pi_t)
\left(
\widehat{\mathcal I}_{t-1}+\widehat\Delta_t
\right)
+\pi_tZ_t.
$$

The independently configured gain $\alpha_t$ belongs only to historical
schema-v4 through schema-v6 baselines. Do not use it in a principal Phase 8
run. Such a run must choose $\pi_t$ from information available before the
step-$t$ batch, then use that one value for both the Fisher update and EWC
objective.

After updating the auxiliary process, use the current observations to calculate the original process's proposed move $\widetilde u_t$, apply any configured controller, and move to $\theta_{t+1}$. The LFU at step $t$ must use the realized prior displacement $u_{t-1}$, not the new proposal.

At $t=0$, use the high-quality initial Fisher and define the unavailable preceding correction as zero.

## Fisher-estimator conditions

Implement these dense conditions first:

- `ema`: set $\widehat\Delta_t=0$;
- `ac_only`: use $\widehat\Delta_t=\widehat\Delta_{C,t}$;
- `full_lfu`: use $\widehat\Delta_t=\widehat\Delta_{C,t}+\widehat\Delta_{R,t}$;
- `periodic_fresh`: use the configured online estimator between checkpoints and replace it with a high-sample fresh Fisher at a configured cadence.

The high-sample reference Fisher is a measurement oracle, not an ordinary low-compute condition. EMA is the primary experimental control. Periodic fresh replacement is an expensive oracle-like learning condition.

Record the AC and residual contributions separately even when the active condition does not use both. This is necessary to determine whether $R$ is materially nonzero and whether either component dominates variance.

## Fixed-trajectory tracking

The first estimator comparison must isolate Fisher tracking from learning feedback.

For every replica, produce one canonical parameter trajectory using a treatment-independent driver. The default driver should use the high-sample/reference Fisher in the EWC proposal at configured refresh points. Store every $\theta_t$, realized $u_t$, observation identifier, and driver setting.

Replay that immutable trajectory through every Fisher-estimator condition. Estimators may observe the path but may not alter it. Call this the **fixed-trajectory tracking experiment**.

This stage answers:

- Does full LFU predict Fisher motion better than EMA?
- Does the residual improve over the AC-only correction?
- How do step resolution and per-step sample size affect tracking?
- How much instability is hidden by PSD projection?

## EWC-coupled learning

After fixed-trajectory tracking passes its validation criteria, run the **EWC-coupled learning experiment**. Each condition uses its own Fisher estimate in the EWC penalty, so it affects the next estimate and all future parameters.

Conditions still share initialization, $p$ schedule, observation identities, and non-treatment optimizer settings, but their parameter paths may diverge. Do not compare Fisher matrices from divergent paths as though they were estimates at the same point. Evaluate each estimate against a high-sample reference Fisher calculated at that condition's own checkpoint.

This stage answers whether improved Fisher tracking produces better retention, adaptation, and compute efficiency.

## High-sample reference Fisher

At configured checkpoints, estimate

$$
\widehat{\mathcal I}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta)
=
\frac1{N_{\mathrm{ref}}}
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{ref}}}
s(X_i;\theta)s(X_i;\theta)^T
$$

from an independent holdout sampled at the checkpoint's configured $p$. Make `reference_sample_size` configurable. Calculate reference estimates in chunks so peak memory does not scale with $N_{\mathrm{ref}}$.

Use convergence checks rather than assuming one sample size is adequate. At selected checkpoints, compare nested or paired reference estimates at increasing $N_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and record their relative disagreement. Schema-v8 controller runs additionally treat each independently sampled chunk mean as a matrix-valued observation, maintain its Welford variance in Frobenius geometry, and stop only when the configured six-sigma radius is below its absolute-plus-relative tolerance or the hard sample ceiling is reached. Record the radius, threshold, stopping reason, duplicate fraction, adjacent-chunk index overlap, and lag-one Frobenius correlation.

Reference-optimum paths in schema v8 use at least eight independently sampled warm-started fit trajectories. Measure the Euclidean confidence radius of every adjacent displacement directly, add fit trajectories until it meets the same configured tolerance or reaches the fit cap, and average the completed fit trajectories to define the oracle path. Store independent validation-score confidence balls and training/validation overlap diagnostics for every fit. A path that reaches a hard ceiling is a valid immutable artifact but fails the scientific oracle gate.

Cache reference matrices using keys that include the model checkpoint hash, holdout-sampler identity, sample count, parameter ordering, and dtype.

## Score-only finite-difference validation

Validate the LFU at interior points $p_\star\in(0,1)$. Let $\theta_\star$ be the selected checkpoint and let $v$ be a normalized realized trajectory direction. Estimate the central directional derivative using only score-based reference Fishers:

$$
\widehat{D\mathcal I}_{\theta_\star}^{\,\mathrm{stencil}}[v]
=
\frac{
\widehat{\mathcal I}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta_\star+\epsilon v)
-
\widehat{\mathcal I}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta_\star-\epsilon v)
}{2\epsilon}.
$$

Use the same holdout observations at both stencil points to reduce Monte Carlo variance. Test a configurable grid of $\epsilon$ values and reference sample sizes. Reject stencil settings dominated by either Monte Carlo noise or finite-difference truncation.

Also compare the actual high-sample Fisher increment

$$
\widehat{\mathcal I}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta_t)
-
\widehat{\mathcal I}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\theta_{t-1})
$$

with the AC-only and full lagged LFU predictions. Do not run stencils at $p=0$ or $p=1$.

## PSD projection

For a dense candidate $A_t$, symmetrize and project after the complete EMA/LFU blend:

$$
S_t=\frac12(A_t+A_t^T),
$$

$$
\widehat{\mathcal I}_t
=
\Pi_{\mathrm{PSD}}(S_t)
=
Q\operatorname{diag}(\max(\lambda_i,0))Q^T.
$$

This is the nearest PSD matrix in Frobenius norm. Since the reference Fisher is PSD, projection cannot increase its Frobenius distance to that target.

Projection must not hide instability. Before projection, record:

- minimum eigenvalue;
- count of negative eigenvalues;
- negative spectral mass;
- Frobenius projection distance;
- correction norm relative to the pre-projection estimate.

PSD is sufficient for an EWC quadratic but not for inversion. Legacy representation diagnostics may use explicitly damped solves, but Phase 8 controllers must not invert or pseudoinvert an observed Fisher matrix.

For diagonal representations, projection is elementwise clipping at zero. Low-rank-plus-diagonal projection is described below.

## Matrix representations

Restrict the representation experiment to:

1. `dense`;
2. `diagonal`;
3. `low_rank_diagonal`.

The diagonal condition stores and updates only the diagonal entries of $Z_t$, $\widehat\Delta_{C,t}$, and $\widehat\Delta_{R,t}$.

Represent the low-rank-plus-diagonal condition as

$$
\widehat{\mathcal I}=AA^T+\operatorname{diag}(d),
\qquad d_i\geq0.
$$

Use the copied implementation in `src/lanczos.py` through a wrapper; preserve that copied module rather than modifying it during integration. It was copied from another project with behavior-preserving whitespace normalization. The wrapper may provide deterministic seeds, matrix-vector products, device handling, validation, and artifact conversion.

The updated Fisher must be available as a symmetric matrix-vector operator. Use Lanczos to retain positive Ritz components and calculate a clipped nonnegative residual diagonal. Compare multiple configured ranks, including a degenerate diagonal-only rank and enough ranks to expose the accuracy/compute curve.

Record:

- requested and realized rank;
- Ritz values;
- residual diagonal statistics;
- reconstruction error against the dense projected estimate when available;
- matrix-vector approximation error on fixed probe vectors;
- runtime and peak memory.

Do not run every rank against every earlier experimental factor. First identify informative dense settings, then run a targeted rank grid.

## EWC proposal

At each step, calculate the EWC-regularized estimate from the current data and
the active Fisher representation. Let $\pi_t\in(0,1]$ denote the effective
new-data weight. With a mean-reduced new-data loss, optimize the equivalent
old-to-new odds form

$$
\overline L_{\mathrm{new},t}(\vartheta)
+
\frac{\lambda_t}{2}
(\vartheta-\theta_t)^T
\widehat{\mathcal I}_t
(\vartheta-\theta_t),
\qquad
\lambda_t
=
\gamma\frac{1-\pi_t}{\pi_t},
$$

where $\gamma$ is a separately recorded dimensionless EWC sensitivity
multiplier and is one in principal runs. Accept the optimizer's estimate
directly. Do not multiply its displacement by $\pi_t$ after optimization.
The exact optimizer and inner-step count must be configuration-controlled and
identical across paired conditions unless they are the treatment.

Record:

- unregularized data loss;
- EWC penalty;
- $\pi_t$, $\lambda_t$, and objective normalization;
- optimizer displacement norm $\|u_t\|$;
- Fisher-weighted displacement norm.

The LFU on the following iteration uses the realized optimizer displacement
$u_t=\theta_{t+1}-\theta_t$.

## Optimal-controller experiment

Run controller experiments only after the main dense and representation experiments identify a manageable set of Fisher conditions.

Keep these quantities distinct:

- $p_t$: environmental digit-mixture proportion;
- $\pi_t$: the new-observation composition weight, which jointly controls
  adaptation and summary forgetting;
- $m_t$: the fixed number of new observations available at step $t$;
- $N_{\mathrm{eff},t}=q_t^{-1}$: the effective size implied by the accepted
  sequence of controller weights.

For the applied fixed-batch model, use the local risk

$$
R_t(\pi)
=
(1-\pi)^2
\left(\|d\theta_t\|^2+\tau_{\mathrm{old},t}\right)
+\pi^2\tau_{\mathrm{new},t},
$$

with

$$
\tau_{\mathrm{old},t}=\frac{T_t}{N_{\mathrm{eff},t-1}},
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{new},t}=\frac{T_t}{m_t},
\qquad
T_t=\operatorname{tr}\mathcal I(\theta_t)^{-1}.
$$

Its locally optimal fixed-batch controller is

$$
\widehat\pi_t^\star
=
\frac{
\|\widehat d\theta_t\|^2+\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{old},t}
}{
\|\widehat d\theta_t\|^2+\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{old},t}
+\widehat\tau_{\mathrm{new},t}
}.
$$

Estimate the local trend with a vector EMA of accepted displacements and
estimate $T_t$ with the scalar residual moment described in
`mathematical_overview.ipynb`. The principal defaults are a trend half-life of
$0.20$ in environmental $p$-distance, $\pi_{\min}=0.05$, and
$\pi_{\max}=0.95$. During the first trend half-life, use the bounded cold-start
composition $m_t/(N_{\mathrm{eff},t-1}+m_t)$. Never use the current batch to
choose its own $\pi_t$.

After accepting the step, update

$$
q_t=(1-\pi_t)^2q_{t-1}+\frac{\pi_t^2}{m_t},
\qquad N_{\mathrm{eff},t}=q_t^{-1}.
$$

The trace moment uses

$$
r_t=u_t-\widehat d\theta_{t|t-1},
\qquad
a_t=\pi_t^2\left(q_{t-1}+m_t^{-1}\right),
\qquad
\widehat T_t=\frac{\operatorname{EMA}(\|r_t\|^2)}
{\operatorname{EMA}(a_t)+\varepsilon}.
$$

Implement these controller policies:

- `uncontrolled`: $\pi_t=1$, a diagnostic new-data-only boundary with no EWC
  penalty;
- `fixed`: $\pi_t=\pi_0$, used for unified fixed-weight baselines;
- `optimal_plugin`: $\pi_t=\widehat\pi_t^\star$;
- `optimal_oracle`: substitute high-sample reference-path displacements and
  an oracle-trend residual moment from the accepted parameter series to
  diagnose trend error without inverting a Fisher matrix;
- `freeze`: $\pi_t=0$, an explicit diagnostic boundary that bypasses ordinary
  controller clipping.

Expose `fixed_pi`, `pi_min`, `pi_max`, `trend_half_life_p`, and controller
epsilon as configuration values. Clamp ordinary plug-in and oracle policies to
$[\pi_{\min},\pi_{\max}]$. Test targeted bound and trend-half-life grids only
after selecting the principal Fisher conditions.

Record the raw and applied value, both bound activations, EWC odds, trend and
oracle displacement norms, plug-in and oracle-residual trace estimates, $q_t$, effective size, cold-start
status, and intervention outcome at every step. Choosing $\pi_t$ adaptively
generally targets a tempered objective and may bias the estimate relative to
the instantaneous MLE. Treat that bias as an explicit
variance-retention-tracking trade-off. Also report the old fixed-$N$ Bernoulli
oracle from the theoretical model as a diagnostic only; it is not the applied
controller.

## Metrics

Compute expensive metrics in the execution script and store tidy per-step scalar results. The results notebook should only aggregate them.

### Fisher tracking

- absolute and relative Frobenius error to the high-sample reference;
- AC-only and full-LFU one-step prediction error before the direct EMA observation;
- directional error $u^T(\widehat{\mathcal I}-\widehat{\mathcal I}_{\mathrm{ref}})u$;
- operator-norm error when affordable;
- leading eigenspace and eigenvalue error;
- AC norm, residual norm, and their alignment;
- stencil disagreement across epsilon and reference sample size;
- all PSD-projection diagnostics.

### Continual learning

- accuracy and negative log likelihood on digits 0 through 8;
- accuracy and negative log likelihood on digit 9;
- overall balanced accuracy;
- calibration metrics;
- Fisher-weighted parameter drift;
- loss and accuracy immediately before and after each accepted update;
- forgetting relative to each replica's initial performance.

### Compute

- wall-clock time by operation;
- HVP count;
- score-gradient count;
- reference-score count;
- peak CPU and accelerator memory;
- artifact size;
- PSD projection and Lanczos time.

### Controller

- predicted trend, realized optimizer update, and oracle step norms;
- $\widehat\pi_t^\star$ and applied $\pi_t$;
- effective EWC odds $(1-\pi_t)/\pi_t$;
- lower- and upper-bound activation frequency;
- residual and scale EMA moments, $\widehat T_t$, $q_t$, and
  $N_{\mathrm{eff},t}$;
- fixed-batch oracle and theoretical fixed-$N$ Bernoulli-oracle disagreement;
- trend centering, covariance-shape, scalar moment, and local-stationarity
  assumption checks;
- tracking/retention outcomes conditional on controller intervention.

## Staged implementation

1. **Mathematical validation**
   - Implement parameter flattening, per-sample scores, HVPs, AC, residual, and full LFU.
   - Verify $UBU^T$ and HVP identities with unit tests.
   - Validate dense LFU against score-only central stencils at interior $p$.

2. **Dense fixed-trajectory tracking**
   - Implement the canonical model and paired trajectory artifacts.
   - Compare EMA, AC-only, full LFU, and periodic fresh conditions.
   - Vary `num_p_steps` and `samples_per_step`.

3. **Dense EWC-coupled learning**
   - Allow each Fisher condition to influence its own path.
   - Measure retention, adaptation, tracking at each path's own checkpoints, and compute.

4. **Representation study**
   - Implement diagonal and low-rank-plus-diagonal conditions.
   - Evaluate a targeted rank grid on settings selected from dense results.

5. **Optimal-controller study**
   - Introduce schema v7 so one predictable $\pi_t$ drives both EWC and the
     Fisher-summary recursion.
   - Compare uncontrolled, unified fixed, plug-in optimal, freeze-diagnostic,
     and oracle-diagnostic policies.
   - Evaluate targeted $\pi_{\min}$, $\pi_{\max}$, and trend-half-life grids
     only on selected Fisher representations.
   - Preserve schema-v6 decoupled fixed-weight runs as historical diagnostics,
     never as principal controller baselines.

6. **Replication**
   - Accumulate immutable paired replicas.
   - Use the results notebook to decide when uncertainty is sufficiently narrow.
